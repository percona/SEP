MySQL and MongoDB restores can no longer be scheduled. Both restore apps stop offering scheduling, which hides every schedule control for them in the SEP UI and in the SEP UI embedded in PMM. The SEP periodic-task gateway now answers `400 Bad Request` to `POST /api/sep/periodic-tasks/{task_name}/` and `PUT /api/sep/periodic-tasks/{id}` when the schedule would run a task whose app does not offer scheduling — restores included, and likewise a task whose owner matches no installed app or is the default `ANY` — so a restore schedule can no longer be created, edited, re-enabled or disabled through that gateway. Listing and deleting schedules are unchanged, and the periodic routes of the Tasks service itself (`/api/tasks/`) are not guarded. Existing schedules of installed apps that do not offer scheduling (today the two restore apps) are not deleted: each is switched off at the first SEP startup after the upgrade, not at install time, logged at warning level by name, and stays stored until deleted. A running Celery beat stops firing it on its next schedule check, without a restart; until that SEP startup it keeps firing. A restore app already removed from `SEP.APPS` is not swept, so its schedules keep their state.
